French Drain vs. Catch Basin Surface Drain: Which Solves Yard Flooding Better?

By Molly Perkins · Updated 2026-11-21

Installing a grated catch basin in the middle of a soggy clay lawn leaves mud squishing around your ankles for weeks, while burying a gravel French drain under a low concrete patio corner can clog with mulch leaves and overflow during heavy winter cloudbursts. Understanding surface drains versus subsurface gravel trenches stops flooding the first time.

How French Drains and Catch Basins Work Differently

A catch basin collects visible standing surface water directly through a slotted top grate into solid smooth pipe, whereas a French drain sits underground wrapped in woven geotextile fabric and crushed drain rock to pull invisible water out of saturated clay soil.

Drainage System Captures What Water? Pipe Type Used Best Yard Location
Subsurface French Drain Slow underground clay saturation Rigid perforated PVC + filter fabric Wet lawns, retaining walls, hillsides
Catch Basin / Channel Drain Fast visible surface puddles Solid SDR-35 / Schedule 40 PVC Patios, driveways, roof downspouts
Hybrid Dual-Pipe Trench Both roof runoff + soggy clay soil 2 pipes (1 solid + 1 perforated) Most Sacramento perimeter side yards

Which Drainage System Is Right for Your Yard?

Most flooded side yards across Roseville, Folsom, and Elk Grove actually need a hybrid two-pipe trench: one solid smooth PVC line carrying dirty roof downspout and catch-basin water straight to the street curb, and a second perforated French drain pipe beside it in washed drain rock pulling soggy groundwater away from footings.

Never connect leafy roof downspouts into perforated French drain gravel pipe, because roof grit and oak leaves pack the gravel voids solid within three winters.

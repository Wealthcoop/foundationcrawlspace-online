Foundation Crack Repair Guide: Vertical vs. Horizontal Cracks, Causes & Repair Costs

By Molly Perkins · Updated 2026-11-21

Almost every concrete slab and perimeter stem wall in California develops tiny curing cracks during its first few years, yet a growing diagonal, wider-than-a-nickel, or horizontal crack means soil movement is pulling your home's concrete apart. Knowing how to read crack direction and displacement keeps you from ignoring structural movement or overpaying for cosmetic patching.

Which Foundation Cracks Are Serious and Which Are Normal?

Uniform hairline vertical cracks under 1/8 inch wide come from normal concrete drying shrinkage, whereas horizontal fractures, widening diagonal cracks above 1/4 inch, or slab cracks where one side drops lower than the other point directly to structural stress.

Vertical, Diagonal, Horizontal, and Stair-Step Cracks

Look closely at crack shape and location: 1) Vertical shrinkage cracks: Thin hairline splits running straight down a stem wall with zero offset usually need simple sealant to keep moisture off steel rebar. 2) Diagonal & stair-step cracks: Gaps widening at the top near corners or stepping along exterior stucco and brick joints mean a corner footing is sinking into soft clay. 3) Horizontal cracks: Sideways fractures along stem walls or retaining walls show dangerous lateral soil pressure bending the concrete inward.

Crack Pattern Primary Cause Recommended Fix Typical Local Cost
Hairline Vertical (< 1/8 in) Normal concrete curing shrinkage Surface polyurethane sealer or epoxy $450 to $800 per crack
Wider Structural Crack (Dry) Footing tension or corner stress Low-pressure structural epoxy injection $600 to $1,200 per crack
Active Leaking / Wet Crack Hydrostatic groundwater entry Expanding polyurethane resin ports $650 to $1,100 per crack
Offset Slab Drop (> 1/4 in) Deep expansive clay settlement Helical/push piers + crack stitch $6,500 to $22,000+

What Is the Best Way to Fix Foundation Cracks?

Contractors repair dry structural cracks with high-strength structural epoxy that bonds concrete stronger than the original pour, while flexible expanding polyurethane fills damp cracks to lock out water without becoming brittle.

When Crack Injection Is Not Enough

If one side of a garage or living room slab sits noticeably lower than the other side of a crack, glue alone cannot lift concrete or stop clay settlement. Active vertical offset means the soil beneath that perimeter beam needs deep steel underpinning piers before stitching cracks closed.

Can I Repair a Foundation Crack Myself & What Does It Cost?

Surface caulk and tub hydraulic cement fail because they bridge only the top skin while leaving a hollow void inside the wall; pro port injection fills the full 8-to-12-inch thickness for $550 to $950 per crack.

Check your home's garage floor and perimeter stem walls after both wet January storms and dry August heat. Verify contractor licensing on California CSLB and see our complete foundation repair guide when comparing multiple crack bids.
